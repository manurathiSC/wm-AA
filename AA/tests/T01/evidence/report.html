What would move this forward

  • More books, not more settings. Per book, this design can only detect a timing edge of 12–18 pp a year (2.8 × the spread of the random-timing draws). Running the slower rules on many more smallcases' published series is the only way to see a 5 pp edge.
  • Ask the relative question. "Is the smallcase doing well" is mostly about its selection versus its benchmark. A rule on smallcase ÷ Nifty 50 would say whether to switch to the index, not to gold — untested here.
  • The market-index rule is still the stronger lead, though regime-bound: on these same mechanics it beat random timing in 4 of 4 books in 2021–26 and 0 of 4 in 2015–21, and adding your percentile gate on top did not improve it.

Caveats. Before 2021-11 the parents are the backtest harness's rebuild from their real baskets (price return, about 0.5–3.3 pp a year below the published series where both exist), and every series here had been looked at before this test, so no period is unseen. The index has closes only, so trades are at the next session's close, not its OHLC4. Gold's 2024–26 rally flatters any rule that sits in the sleeve then; a cash-only sleeve fails the same tests. CAGR here counts 252 sessions a year (the repo's backtest convention); NSE has about 247, so these levels run 0.2–0.5 pp above a calendar-year CAGR (holding 0042 in 2021–26: 15.30% here, 14.96% on calendar years). The final-strategy page uses calendar years, so compare levels across the two pages with that in mind; comparisons within this page are unaffected.
