## 1. Idea and references

**The idea in one sentence.** Holding a proxy portfolio of the Nifty 100, Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100 only when the three weekly supertrends are all BUY, and otherwise a GOLDBEES and cash sleeve, (a) chooses *when* to hold the portfolio better than random in-or-out timings that keep the rule's switch count and weeks invested (with a fixed 50/50 sleeve, Part A, and with a sleeve split by the MAUc1 gold supertrend, Part B), and (b) times the gold-versus-cash split better than random gold timings that keep the gold signal's switch count and BUY weeks (Part B), on 2011-11-25 → 2019-01-02.

**Where it came from.** T06 (fixed 50/50 sleeve) and T08 (MAUc1-timed sleeve) run on the four real parents, whose baskets and stock prices start in 2019, so neither can say anything about earlier years. The index prices, the three-index signals, the MAUc1 signal, GOLDBEES's proxy and cash now exist for earlier years, so the rule can be run on a proxy: the same three indices the rule already reads, held at each book's own average market-cap mix. The proxy has no stock selection in it, so what a pass shows is timing alone.

**The decision this will change.** Whether the F-AA-S04 / S07 finding (the switch beats chance, PROVISIONAL) and the open question of T08 (does the MAUc1 timing of the sleeve beat random gold timing) hold up on years the parents' backtests never covered. A pass is supporting evidence only: the proxy is not a book anyone held, and the 2014–2019 part of the window was seen.

**What this test is NOT asking.**
- Whether the parents' own stock selection helps or hurts (T07's question) or whether a different rule, signal, sleeve or weighting would do better. There is one rule per part and no search.
- Whether the proxy reproduces the real books. No check of that is part of the test (an optional check, R8 of the plan, was declined). The mixes come from 2019–2026 holdings and are an approximation of what the books would have held earlier.
- Whether the rule beats holding the portfolio. That is a comparator (§3), not a null.
- Whether the rule has skill on weeks nobody has seen. 2011-11-25 → 2014-06-30 predates the look at the rule; the rest does not.
- Whether gold goes up when MAUc1 is BUY. C3 and C4 ask whether the rule's particular timing of the split beats random timing with the same number of switches and BUY weeks, as seen through the rule's own OUT weeks.

**Robustness null F5 (both random):** an F1 draw of the in-or-out states with an F3 draw of the gold states, independent. Reported only (R5).

**The rule is inside every allowed set.** Its own lengths lie within the bounds by construction, so every N4.9 draw is one configuration of N4.10 and every N4.9-g draw one configuration of N4.10-g. Gate V3 checks both.

**Which null decides.** F1 decides A1, A2, C1 and C2; F3 decides C3 and C4. F2 and F4 are reported beside them. If a pair disagrees, both are reported in the verdict and the finding's status note says so (AGENT.md N6.8).

**Statistic the null is judged on.** After-cost CAGR for A1, C1 and C3, higher better. Maximum drawdown on daily NAV for A2, C2 and C4, shallower better.
- **Unit:** one realisation is part × book × cost × null. The percentile is computed per realisation against its own draws.
- **Percentile:** (draws worse + 0.5 × draws tied) ÷ draws × 100. Ties are equal within a relative 1e-12.
- **p-value:** (1 + draws at least as good) ÷ (draws + 1). It is reported beside the percentile; the pass mark is on the percentile.
- **Secondary statistics**, each with its own percentile against the same draws: volatility of daily returns × √252 (lower better); Ulcer index (lower better); days at least 10% below the running peak (fewer better); longest time under water (shorter better); Calmar = CAGR ÷ |maximum drawdown|.

**Pass mark and draws.** The percentile must be at least 90. 2,000 draws give a resolution of 0.05 points and a smallest p-value of 1/2001; (D + 1) ≥ m ÷ α is met for the six criteria (m = 6, α = 0.10).

**Construction, in order.** Steps 1 to 12 follow T08 §3 (steps 1–12) except where stated:

1. **Calendar.** The sessions are D26's dates from T0 = 2011-11-25 to **2019-01-02**. The engine asserts every session has a bar in all three index files and in the cash file and a GOLDBEES bar (D09 or D09b) on it or an as-of level for cash.
2. **Decision dates.** T0, then the last D26 session of each ISO week after T0. T0 is the window's base. The decision at the window's last session (2019-01-02, a Wednesday) is made but not traded.
3. **Signals at decision date d.** Read the latest Master row of each of the three indices dated on or before d (Smallcap 100: dated 2011-11-25 or later). The decision is IN if SIGN is BUY on all three, else OUT. Read the latest MAUc1 Master row dated on or before d; g_d = BUY or SELL. A week whose Master row is dated after its last D26 session uses the previous week's row, one week stale and never ahead of d. Every row is a backfill by the production code.
4. **Targets.** IN: the proxy at the book's weights (three index positions). OUT, Part A: 50% GOLDBEES and 50% cash. OUT, Part B: **w_d of the sleeve in GOLDBEES and 1 − w_d in cash, w_d = 0.8 if g_d is BUY and 0.2 if g_d is SELL.** The two parts share the draws of the in-or-out nulls; Part A is the engine's `fixed50` mode.
5. **Initial position.** NAV is 1.000 at T0's close; the book holds at that close what the decision at T0 says (Part B: the sleeve at w_{T0} when OUT). No cost is charged for this initial position, in any realisation including the nulls.
6. **Execution.** A trade happens on the **next D26 session after d**, at that session's OHLC4 of each instrument (index: (open + high + low + close) ÷ 4 of its D25 row; GOLDBEES: D09b's OHLC4 on sessions before 2014-07-01 (the proxy is MAUc1 itself, so nothing to de-spike), and from 2014-07-01 the de-spiked OHLC4, high and low clipped to MCX gold's range that day, scaled, plus 0.5%, with the MCX range read from D24b's daily file; cash: the level on that session, as-of). Cost: ow = cost_bps ÷ 2 ÷ 10,000 per side on every value traded (index, GOLDBEES, cash). The target changes in four ways:
   - **Exit** (IN → OUT): sell the three index positions; buy GOLDBEES and cash at the split of the proceeds after cost (Part B: w_d for the signal read at the exit decision).
   - **Entry** (OUT → IN): sell the sleeve; buy the three index positions at the book's weights of the proceeds after cost.
   - **Sleeve rebalance** (Part B, OUT on both sides, g changes): sell and buy GOLDBEES and cash from their drifted values to the split w_d of the sleeve value after cost; cost on the value traded. Between events the sleeve is not rebalanced and its weights drift.
   - **Quarterly reset** (IN): at the first session of each calendar quarter (January, April, July, October) after T0, **if the book holds the proxy at the start of that session**, sell and buy the three index positions from their drifted values to the book's weights of the proxy value after cost, at that session's OHLC4; cost on the value traded (the sum of the absolute changes). A session that is also an exit or entry trade session does that trade only. No reset while OUT.
   - Post-cost targets solve the fixed point value' = value − ow × traded exactly (the sleeve rebalance of T08 and the reset are piecewise linear; the engine's `trade()` solves them). A flip of g while IN is not a trade; the split is read at the next exit decision. A decision that is both an exit and a flip is one exit trade at the new g.
7. **Index positions.** Each index position holds units; units are fixed between trades; weights drift. The indices are price indices (no dividends) and are assumed tradable at OHLC4 at the stated cost.
8. **NAV.** Σ index units × close + GOLDBEES units × close + cash units × level. It is 1.000 at T0's close. No sale at the end.
9. **Statistics.** Sessions run from T0 to the last session. years = (sessions − 1) ÷ 252. CAGR = NAV_end^(1/years) − 1. Maximum drawdown, Ulcer index, days at least 10% below the running peak, longest time under water (sessions), volatility = SD of daily returns × √252, Calmar = CAGR ÷ |maximum drawdown|.
10. **Comparators** (benchmarks, not nulls), on the same calendar, cost and T0: hold the proxy (the engine with the decision fixed at IN, at 0 cost and at each cost); the rule with the sleeve fixed at 50/50; the constant mix (the strategy's average share f = invested weeks ÷ weeks held in the proxy, with the rest 50/50 GOLDBEES and cash, and with the rest split by MAUc1 as the timed rule, rebalanced at each decision date's next session at OHLC4 with the same cost, including after T0's decision); the 100% sleeve at 50/50 held throughout and the 100% sleeve timed by MAUc1; each single index held at 0 cost and at each cost.
11. **Null draws.** From the in-or-out series s_0 … s_n (n = 371), cut maximal spells: 12 in-spells and 13 out-spells. From the gold series g_0 … g_n, cut maximal spells: 15 BUY spells and 14 SELL spells.
    - **N4.9 spell shuffle of the in-or-out series (F2):** shuffle the in-spells among themselves and the out-spells among themselves with `numpy.random.default_rng([12, 3, draw_id])` (draw_id = 0 … 1,999), interleave starting in the rule's first state (OUT).
    - **N4.10 free-length draw of the in-or-out series (F1):** k_in = 12, S_in = 172, bounds [1, 32]; k_out = 13, S_out = 200, bounds [2, 35]. Let c(m, t) be the number of ways to write t as m integers in [lo, hi]: c(0, 0) = 1, c(0, t) = 0 for t > 0, c(m, t) = Σ_{x = lo..hi} c(m − 1, t − x). The parts are drawn one at a time: with m parts and t weeks left, P(length = x) ∝ c(m − 1, t − x) for x in [lo, hi]. This is exactly uniform over the c(k, S) ordered tuples, with no draw rejected. *Worked example (k = 3, S = 12, bounds [2, 6], 19 tuples):* the first part is 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 with probabilities 3, 4, 5, 4, 3 over 19. The uniform number comes from `rng.random()` and a cumulative-sum search (never `Generator.choice`), from `numpy.random.default_rng([12, 6, draw_id])`, in-spell lengths first and then out-spell lengths from the same generator. The tuples are exactly 10,354,725,991,095,786 (in) and 480,915,680,540,493,952 (out); the engine prints the counts for every window.
    - **N4.9-g spell shuffle of the gold series (F4):** shuffle the BUY spells among themselves and the SELL spells among themselves with `numpy.random.default_rng([12, 11, 3, draw_id])`, interleave starting in the real first gold state (BUY).
    - **N4.10-g free-length draw of the gold series (F3):** the same sampler with k_buy = 15, S_buy = 190, bounds [2, 36]; k_sell = 14, S_sell = 182, bounds [2, 27]; BUY lengths first, then SELL lengths, from `numpy.random.default_rng([12, 11, 6, draw_id])`; interleaved starting in BUY. The bounds are the real series' shortest spell (2) and longest BUY and SELL spells (36, 27), computed before any return was read. The tuples are exactly 76,727,187,438,249,573,981 (BUY) and 693,114,838,376,918,505 (SELL).
    - **F5:** an F1 draw (seed `[12, 6, draw_id]`) and an F3 draw (seed `[12, 11, 6, draw_id]`) of the same draw id, run together.
    - The same draw ids are used for every cost and every book (the four books' series are identical). The R3 window has its own spells, totals and bounds from the same seeds: in-or-out in [1, 32] (k 12, S 172) and out [2, 35] (k 13, S 195); BUY [2, 36] (k 14, S 187), SELL [2, 27] (k 14, S 180).
    - Each draw runs through the same engine (steps 5 to 9) with the same cost: in F1, F2 and F5 the draw's in-or-out states with the real (F1, F2) or drawn (F5) gold states; in F3 and F4 the real in-or-out states with the drawn gold states. The draws' index trades use the real index prices and the same quarterly resets; only the states differ. Every F1 and F2 draw is also run with the sleeve fixed at 50/50 (the null of A1 and A2, and R6). The states of every draw are saved (`draw_states.npz`, in-or-out and gold), and `rerun_draw.py` reproduces any draw from its id.
12. **MDE.** For A1, C1 and C3, the smallest timing edge detectable at 80% power and 5% two-sided is 2.8 × null_sd, where null_sd is the SD of after-cost CAGR across the draws of a realisation (per null). For A2, C2 and C4 it is 2.8 × the SD of maximum drawdown. Both are printed beside every FAIL. **Disclosed:** the numbers did not exist when this section was written; the thresholds in §2 do not depend on them. T06's N4.10 values (null SD of CAGR 5.4 to 6.9 pp, MDE 15 to 19 pp) are the prior for A1; for C3 and C4 there is no prior.

**How the test works, in plain words.**

- **The series.** The portfolio is three indices held in fixed proportions (for SCMO_0003, 9.6% Nifty 100, 19.7% Midcap 100 and 70.7% Smallcap 100). Every Friday three weekly index signals are read. If all three say BUY, the book holds the three indices at those proportions, set again at the start of every quarter. Otherwise it holds a sleeve of GOLDBEES and cash. In Part A the sleeve is always half and half. In Part B a fourth signal, gold's own weekly supertrend (MAUc1), sets the split: 80% GOLDBEES and 20% cash when it says BUY, 20% and 80% when it says SELL, changed only when that signal changes. A switch is made on the next session at the (open + high + low + close) ÷ 4 of each instrument, paying 10.4 bps on every rupee traded. *[the real signals, trades and NAVs are printed in §4.0a]*
- **One decision.** On a Friday, look up the latest weekly row of the three index Master files and of the MAUc1 Master file. If the three index SIGN cells are all BUY, the target is the portfolio; otherwise the target is the sleeve at the split the sleeve rule gives. The trade, if the target changed, is on the next session. *[printed in §4.0a]*
- **The result of one path.** Units of each index, GOLDBEES and cash are fixed between trades. Each day the NAV is Σ units × close. The CAGR and drawdown come from that daily NAV. *[printed in §4.0a]*
- **One draw of the in-or-out nulls (F1, F2).** The real path is 25 spells: 12 in and 13 out. F2 keeps the 25 lengths and shuffles which in-spell goes where and which out-spell goes where. F1 keeps only the *number* of spells and the *total* weeks in and out, and picks twelve new in-spell lengths between 1 and 32 weeks that add up to 172 and thirteen new out-spell lengths between 2 and 35 weeks that add up to 200, every allowed set equally likely. In Part B the real gold signal is then read in whatever weeks the draw is OUT.
- **One draw of the gold nulls (F3, F4).** The real gold signal is 29 spells: 15 BUY and 14 SELL. F4 shuffles which BUY spell goes where and which SELL spell goes where. F3 keeps the number of spells and the total BUY and SELL weeks, and picks fifteen BUY lengths between 2 and 36 weeks adding up to 190 and fourteen SELL lengths between 2 and 27 weeks adding up to 182, every allowed set equally likely, starting BUY. The real in-or-out switch is kept, so the book still holds the portfolio in exactly the rule's weeks, and only the split of its sleeve moves. The first 20 weeks of a real, an F2, an F1, an F4 and an F3 series are printed in §4.0a.
- **The ranking.** The rule's CAGR is placed among the 2,000 draws' CAGRs: the percentile is the share of draws it beats (ties count half); the p-value is the chance of doing as well by luck. 90 or more is a pass.
- **The comparators.** Holding the proxy is what the book would do with no rule. The same rule with the 50/50 sleeve is Part A's rule, so the gap between the two is the sleeve's effect. The constant mixes hold the same average share in the proxy all the time: they show how much of any gain is only a different average exposure.

**Point-in-time rule.** A decision at d reads Master rows (the three indices and MAUc1) dated at or before d only (step 3). A trade is on a session after d. The engine asserts both for every decision, counts them, and writes the counts to `audit.json`. The Master rows were produced afterwards by the production code and are not signals traded at the time, and no MAUc1 row was traded by the desk; this is disclosed, not corrected.

**Execution and cost.** AGENT.md R1 (next-session OHLC4, price return, 10.4 bps a side, weights drift between the stated resets) with these specifics: cash trades pay the cost; the first position at T0 is free; no sale at the end; the proxy's gold fills before 2014-07-01 are the proxy's OHLC4; sleeve rebalances and quarterly resets pay the cost on the value traded.

**Effective sample size and power.** 12 round trips of the in-or-out switch and 11 sleeve trades, shared by the four books; 372 weekly decisions. The independent unit for timing is the round trip (equity) and the gold flip that reaches the sleeve (gold). MDE: step 12, computed after the run from the draws.

**What the nulls control for, and what they do not.** F1 and F2 control for the fraction of weeks in the portfolio and the number of switches (F2 also for every spell's length). They do not control for which weeks the sleeve is held (gold's path in the weeks a draw sits in the sleeve), nor for the real gold signal's state in the draw's OUT weeks. F3 and F4 control for the number of gold switches, the fraction of weeks BUY, and the whole in-or-out series. They do not control for how many of the drawn flips fall in OUT weeks, nor for the persistence of the gold signal. No null controls for the backfill, the proxy's distance from a real book, or for the 80/20 weights being fixed after earlier looks. R6 and the comparators bound the sleeve's effect.
